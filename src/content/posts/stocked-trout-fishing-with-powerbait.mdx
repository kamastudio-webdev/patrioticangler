Find a pond or lake that was just stocked with rainbow trout, tie on a sliding sinker rig with a small treble hook, and mold a ball of floating PowerBait around the hook. Cast it out, set the rod down and watch the tip. That simple setup catches a lot of trout, and it's an easy way to get kids their first fish.

## Find the stocked trout first

Most states raise rainbow trout in hatcheries and stock them in ponds, lakes and streams, often in spring and fall when the water is cool. Your state wildlife agency posts its stocking schedule or stocking reports, usually online and often listed by lake or stream. Check it before you go, because the first days after a stocking are usually the best.

Fresh stockers tend to stay in groups near where they were released, which is often by a boat ramp, a parking area or an easy stretch of bank. Hatchery trout grow up eating pellets, so a small, bright, smelly bait looks like dinner.

## Why floating bait works

Trout in lakes often cruise a foot or two above the bottom, and a bait lying in mud or weeds is easy to miss. Floating dough bait solves that. A sinker holds the rig on the bottom, and the bait floats up on the leader, right where trout swim by.

Most jarred trout dough is made to float, but check the label. A bait that sinks won't do the job on this rig.

## Gear for stocked trout

- **Rod and reel:** a light or ultralight spinning rod, 5 to 7 feet long, with a small spinning reel. Light gear casts a small rig easily and makes a 12-inch trout fun.
- **Main line:** 4- to 6-pound monofilament.
- **Leader:** 4-pound monofilament.
- **Hooks:** small trebles in size 14 to 18, which hold dough well. A size 10 or 12 single hook works too.
- **Sinkers and swivels:** egg sinkers from 1/8 to 1/4 oz and a few small barrel swivels.

## How to tie the sliding sinker rig

1. Slide an egg sinker onto your main line.
2. Tie the main line to one end of a small barrel swivel. The swivel stops the sinker from sliding down to the hook.
3. Tie 18 to 24 inches of 4-pound line to the other end of the swivel.
4. Tie a size 14 to 18 treble hook to the end of that leader.
5. Pinch off a piece of dough about the size of a pea or small marble, and mold it around the treble until the whole hook is covered.

Use an improved clinch knot or a Palomar knot for each connection. Both are covered in [how to tie the Palomar and improved clinch knots](/palomar-knot-and-improved-clinch-knot/).

Because the line slides through the sinker, a trout can swim off with the bait without feeling much weight. The leader length sets how high the bait floats. Use 18 inches over a clean bottom and 24 inches or a little more over weeds.

No egg sinkers? Pinch one or two split shot on the line 18 to 24 inches above the hook. It doesn't slide, so a trout may feel it more, but it still catches fish.

<Tip>Keep your hands dry and roll the dough between your fingers for a few seconds before you mold it. Warm dough sticks to the hook better and is less likely to fly off on the cast.</Tip>

## Cast, wait and watch

1. Lob the rig out with a smooth, easy cast. A hard snap can throw the bait off the hook.
2. Let the sinker settle on the bottom, then reel up slack until the line is straight and the rod tip bends just slightly.
3. Set the rod in a holder with the tip pointed up a little.
4. Watch the tip. Small taps mean a trout is nosing the bait. When the tip pulls down and stays down, or bounces steadily, pick up the rod and reel while lifting it in a firm, smooth sweep. Don't jerk hard on 4-pound line.
5. Check your bait every 15 minutes or so. Dough loses scent and can wash off, so re-bait and cast to a new spot if nothing's biting.

<Tip>Can't tell a bite from the wind? Clip a small bell to the rod tip, or simply keep your eyes on it. Kids love being the one who spots the first wiggle.</Tip>

## Other baits on the same rig

- **Nightcrawler:** swap the treble for a size 8 or 10 baitholder hook and thread on half a nightcrawler. Some anglers add a pea-size ball of floating dough to lift the worm off the bottom.
- **Small spinner:** when trout are cruising and you want to cover water, cast and reel a small inline spinner. [Trout fishing with inline spinners](/trout-fishing-with-inline-spinners/) walks you through it.
- **Small spoon:** a little metal spoon casts a long way on light line and flashes like a baitfish.

## Keep a few, or let them go

Many stocked waters are stocked so people can keep some trout, but limits and rules vary, so check your state's regulations. Some states also require a trout stamp or permit on top of your fishing license, and some limit how many rods you can fish at once.

Some special waters are artificial-only, fly-only or catch-and-release, or have seasons where bait isn't allowed. You can't use PowerBait there, so read the signs and the rules for that water.

Trout often swallow bait. If you plan to release a fish that's hooked deep, cut the line close to the hook instead of digging it out. Wet your hands before you handle the fish and get it back in the water quickly. Treble hooks are sharp, so unhook fish with pliers and keep little ones a few steps back while someone is casting.

## Cleaning trout for the table

Keep trout cold from the moment you catch them. Put them on ice in a cooler, not in a bucket of water that warms up in the sun. Cleaning a trout takes a couple of minutes:

1. Rinse the fish. Trout scales are tiny, so there's no need to scale them.
2. Slip the knife tip into the vent, the small opening near the tail on the belly, and cut a shallow slit up to the gills.
3. Cut through the narrow strip of skin under the jaw where the gills meet, then pull the gills and guts out together.
4. Run your thumbnail or a spoon along the backbone inside the belly to scrape out the dark bloodline.
5. Rinse in cold water, pat dry and keep the fish cold until you cook it.

Trout are great cooked whole in a pan with butter or on the grill. When they're done, the meat lifts right off the bones. Cook them within a day or two, or freeze them.

## Common questions

### What color PowerBait is best?

There's no single best color. Trout can switch preferences from day to day, so carry two or three colors and change if you aren't getting bites.

### Why do I keep missing bites?

You may be setting the hook too early or using too much bait. Wait for the rod tip to pull down steadily, and keep the dough ball about the size of a pea.

### Does PowerBait work in streams?

It can, but floating bait matters less in moving water. In streams, a nightcrawler, small spinner or spoon is usually easier. Many trout streams also have bait restrictions, so check first.
