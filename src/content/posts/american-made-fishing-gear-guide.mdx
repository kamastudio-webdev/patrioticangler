You can fill most of a tackle box with American-made gear. It's concentrated in a few categories, though: soft plastics, spinners, tackle boxes, line and better rods. Budget reels are the big gap. Most of them are made overseas.

This guide covers what the labels really mean, which brands to look for in each category, and how to build a starter box that's mostly made in the USA.

## What "Made in USA" actually means

The Federal Trade Commission sets the rules. A product labeled Made in USA has to be "all or virtually all" made in the United States. In plain terms, the important parts and the work of making them should be American, with only a tiny amount of foreign content.

Products that don't clear that bar can still use a qualified claim, like "Assembled in USA" or "Made in USA of U.S. and imported parts." Those labels are honest. They just tell you something different.

Online marketplaces are the weak spot. The FTC has warned both Amazon and Walmart about third-party sellers making false Made in USA claims, so a listing that says Made in USA isn't proof on its own.

### How the badges on this site work

Product cards on Patriotic Angler can carry one of two badges:

- **Made in USA:** the maker or the product listing says the product is made in the USA.
- **Assembled in USA:** the product is put together here from imported parts.

No badge doesn't mean a product is imported. It means the origin isn't clear enough to label. Either way, it's worth checking the package before you buy. The country of origin is usually printed on the back or the bottom.

## Soft plastics

Soft plastics are the easiest place to buy American. Several of the best-known brands pour their baits in U.S. plants.

**Zoom Bait Company** is the big name. Ed Chambers founded Zoom in 1977, and the company grew from a single 55-gallon drum into a 40,000-square-foot plant with more than 100 employees. Zoom marks its baits Made in USA.

The Super Fluke is a soft jerkbait you rig weightless and twitch just under the surface. It's a bass bait that's simple enough for a first-timer. Here's the full walkthrough: [how to fish a Zoom Super Fluke](/how-to-fish-a-zoom-super-fluke/).

<Product id="zoom-super-fluke" note="A weightless soft jerkbait that bass hit near the surface. Start with white or pearl on a 4/0 offset worm hook." />

The Zoom Trick Worm is the other Zoom bait worth keeping on hand, and it's also made in the USA. It's a straight-tail worm that works on a Texas rig or wacky rig. See [how to Texas rig a soft plastic worm](/how-to-texas-rig-a-soft-plastic-worm/) for the setup.

Mister Twister's 2-inch Teenie Twister Tail is made in the USA too. It's a small curly-tail grub that bluegill, crappie and small bass will all eat, which makes it a great bait for fishing with kids.

<Product id="mister-twister-teenie" note="A 2-inch curly-tail grub for panfish and crappie. Thread it on a 1/32 or 1/16 oz jighead and reel slowly, or hang it under a bobber." />

A few smaller American lure makers are worth knowing about too: Creme Lures, Santone Lures in Texas, Delta Lures and Venture Lures. Tackle Warehouse marks their baits as Made in USA, so give them a look if your local shop carries them.

## Spinners

**Mepps** spinners are a classic for trout, panfish and bass. Every Mepps spinner sold in the U.S. is hand-assembled in Antigo, Wisconsin. The components come from France, and that's why Mepps gets the Assembled in USA badge here instead of Made in USA.

A fun bit of Mepps history: the company has a long-running program that buys squirrel tails from hunters. The hair goes onto its dressed hooks, the fuzzy tails you see on some Mepps spinners.

<Product id="mepps-classic-aglia-kit" note="An assortment of Aglia spinners, Mepps' classic design. The small sizes, #0 to #2, are the ones to use for stream trout and panfish." />

**Rooster Tail** spinners are another favorite, but retailers don't agree on where they're made. Some list them as Made in USA and others list them as imported, so they don't get a badge here. If it matters to you, check the package. To learn how to fish both, see [trout fishing with inline spinners](/trout-fishing-with-inline-spinners/).

## Line and floats

PowerPro braided line is made in the USA. Braid is thin for its strength and has almost no stretch, which helps in weedy bass water and on long casts. Most beginners do better starting with monofilament, so save braid for when you're comfortable with your reel.

Thill floats are made in the USA as well. They're built for panfish and crappie, where you need to see light bites.

<Product id="thill-americas-favorite" note="A float for bluegill and crappie. Add enough split shot that only the top of the float sits above the water, so even a light bite pulls it under." />

**Berkley** got its start in Spirit Lake, Iowa, in 1937, and it's still one of the biggest names in line and bait. But where each Berkley product is made today isn't published, so Berkley products don't get a badge here.

## Tackle boxes

Tackle boxes are one of the most dependable American-made categories. Flambeau has made tackle boxes in the USA since 1947, in Middlefield, Ohio.

<Product id="flambeau-6382" note="A 2-tray box with room for a beginner's hooks, sinkers, bobbers and a few lures. A good size for a kid's first box." />

Plano's hard tackle boxes are made in Illinois, and the Plano 3-Tray Tackle Box with Dual Top Access is made in the USA. It's the step up when you need more room. Some of Plano's soft tackle bags are made overseas, so check the tag on a bag.

Both boxes are covered in more detail in [best tackle boxes for beginners](/best-tackle-boxes-for-beginners/).

## Rods

**St. Croix** has built rods in Park Falls, Wisconsin, since 1948. Its Premier, Avid, Legend and Victory series are built in Park Falls. Its Triumph, Mojo and Bass-X rods are built at the company's factory in Fresnillo, Mexico.

<Tip>With St. Croix, the series name tells you where the rod was built. If you want one made in Park Falls, look for Premier, Avid, Legend or Victory on the blank.</Tip>

<Product id="st-croix-premier" note="Built in Park Falls, Wisconsin. A 6-foot-6 medium-light rod suits panfish, trout and small bass; a 7-foot medium covers most bass fishing." />

A few other American rod makers to know:

- **Lamiglas** has been building rods in Woodland, Washington, for decades.
- **Sage** builds fly rods on Bainbridge Island, Washington. Sage reels are not made in the USA, so don't assume the whole outfit is.
- **Thomas & Thomas** builds fly rods in Greenfield, Massachusetts.

## Reels: the hard category

Reels are where buying American gets tough. Only a handful of companies build reels in the U.S., and most of them make fly reels or large saltwater reels, not the small spinning reels beginners use. Abel builds fly reels in Montrose, Colorado, and Ross and Dyna-King also make fly reels here.

Popular budget spinning reels, like the Penn Battle II, are made in China.

Here's the honest advice: put your American-made dollars into lures, line, boxes and a rod. Then buy the best reel you can afford, wherever it's made. A smooth drag and a bail that closes every time will help you land more fish than any label. Here's a starter box built from the gear in this guide:

## Common questions

### Is American-made fishing gear better?

Not automatically. A company that builds in its own U.S. plant can often keep a closer eye on quality, and buying from it supports the people who work there. But a well-made imported lure catches fish too. Buy what fits the fishing you do, and pick American-made when it fits.

### How can I check where something is made?

Start with the package, then check the maker's website, which often says where each product line is built. Read the wording closely. "Made in USA," "Assembled in USA" and "Designed in USA" mean three different things. A product designed in the USA was planned here but could be built anywhere.
