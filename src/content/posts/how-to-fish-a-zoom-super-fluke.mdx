Rig a Zoom Super Fluke weightless on a 4/0 EWG hook, twitch your rod tip twice, pause, and repeat. That's the whole technique. The bait darts side to side on each twitch, then glides and slowly sinks on the pause, like a hurt shad or shiner. Bass feeding on baitfish have a hard time passing it up.

## What a soft jerkbait does

A soft jerkbait has no lip, no rattles and no built-in wobble. All the action comes from you. Each snap of the rod tip makes it dart one way or the other, and each pause lets it fall and flutter.

That stop-and-go, zigzag movement looks like an injured baitfish, an easy meal for a bass. And because the hook point is tucked into the plastic, you can work it over submerged grass and around brush without hanging up much.

## When the Super Fluke shines

- **Spring**, as the water warms and bass move into shallow water.
- **Shallow grass**, especially submerged weeds a foot or two under the surface.
- **Post-spawn**, when bass leave their beds and start chasing baitfish again.
- **Schooling fish**, when you see bass busting baitfish on the surface, often in late summer and fall.
- **Docks, fallen trees and points** where baitfish gather.

When bass are holding deep on the bottom, a [Texas-rigged worm](/how-to-texas-rig-a-soft-plastic-worm/) usually gets down to them better.

## How to rig it weightless

You need one hook and nothing else: no weight, no bead. A 4/0 EWG hook fits the standard Super Fluke, which is a little over 5 inches long. Go to 5/0 if the point won't line up with the slot on the back.

1. **Tie the hook to your line or leader.** A Palomar knot works well here. See [how to tie a Palomar knot](/palomar-knot-and-improved-clinch-knot/).
2. **Push the hook point into the center of the nose** about 1/4 inch.
3. **Bring the point out the bottom** of the bait, right where the slot on the belly begins.
4. **Slide the nose up the hook** until it covers the Z bend and sits snug against the eye.
5. **Rotate the hook** so the point faces up toward the bait's back.
6. **Lay the hook alongside the bait** to see where it lines up, then push the point up through the belly slot and out through the slot on the back.
7. **Skin-hook the point.** Tuck the very tip just under the thin layer of plastic in the back slot, so it's hidden but close to the surface.
8. **Hold it up by the line and check it.** It should hang straight, with no bend.

<Tip>If your fluke spins or rolls instead of darting, it's rigged crooked. Pull the hook and re-rig it, and check it again after every fish, since bites tend to push the nose down the hook.</Tip>

## How to work it: twitch, twitch, pause

1. **Cast past where you think the fish are.** Long casts help, since bass in shallow water spook easily.
2. **Point your rod tip down** toward the water at about 4 o'clock. That keeps the bait just under the surface instead of skipping across it.
3. **Give two short, sharp snaps** with the rod tip, moving it only 6 to 12 inches each time. Snap against a little slack in the line, not a tight line. The slack is what lets the bait dart sideways.
4. **Pause for a count of one to three.** Let it glide and sink. Many bites come right here.
5. **Reel up the slack** and repeat.

Change the pause until the fish tell you what they want. Active fish in warm water often hit short pauses. In cooler water, let the bait sink longer, even a foot or two down.

## Watch your line for bites

Bass often grab a fluke on the pause, and you won't always feel it. Keep your eyes on the line where it enters the water. Look for:

- The line jumping or twitching.
- The line swimming off to one side.
- Slack line suddenly going tight.
- A swirl, flash or boil where the bait was.

## Wait a beat before you set the hook

This is the hardest part. When a bass swirls on the bait, your instinct is to jerk right away. Don't. The bait is soft and the hook is tucked inside it, so setting too early often pulls it straight out of the fish's mouth.

Instead, count "one-thousand-one" to give the fish time to close its mouth and turn. Then reel down until you feel weight, and sweep the rod up and to the side, firm and steady.

If you miss, don't reel in fast. Let the bait sit and sink for a second or two. A bass that missed will often come back for it.

## Colors

- **White and pearl.** These look like shad and are easy for bass to see in clear to stained water. They're the best first colors to buy.
- **Darker natural colors.** Watermelon, green pumpkin and smoky baitfish shades look more realistic in very clear water, where bass get a long look at the bait.

A simple rule: match the baitfish. If the lake has shad, start with white.

## Line choice

Two setups both work well.

- **Braid with a leader.** Use 20-pound braid as your main line, with 3 to 4 feet of 12- to 15-pound fluorocarbon or mono tied on as a leader. Braid casts a long way and doesn't stretch, so your hook set reaches the fish even at the end of a long cast. The leader is harder for bass to see. A double uni knot is a common way to join the two lines.
- **Straight monofilament, 10 to 12 pounds.** This is the simpler option, with no extra knot. Mono's stretch is forgiving if you set the hook a little hard, and it floats, which helps keep the bait up high.

Leader tip: mono floats and fluorocarbon sinks. A mono leader keeps the fluke closer to the surface, and a fluorocarbon leader lets it sink deeper on the pause.

## Other ways to rig a fluke

- **Carolina rig.** Slide a 1/2- to 3/4-ounce sinker and a bead onto your main line, tie on a swivel, then add a 2- to 3-foot leader with the fluke on the end. This gets the bait down to bass on deeper points and flats, where it trails behind the weight and drifts down slowly.
- **Belly-weighted hook.** A hook with a small weight molded onto the shank lets the fluke sink faster and stay level. It's handy on windy days or in water 5 to 10 feet deep.

Before you head out, make sure your fishing license is current and check your state's bass size and bag limits. If you're fishing from a boat or kayak, wear a life jacket.

## Common questions

### Do I need a weight?

No. Weightless is the standard way to fish a Super Fluke. Switch to a belly-weighted hook or a Carolina rig only when you need to get deeper or cast into wind.

### What rod works best?

A spinning rod about 6 1/2 to 7 feet long with medium or medium-heavy power is the easiest place to start. It casts a light, weightless bait well and has enough backbone to set the hook.

### Why do I keep missing fish?

Usually it's setting the hook too soon. Wait a beat, reel down, then sweep. If you still miss, check that the point is skin-hooked, not buried deep.
